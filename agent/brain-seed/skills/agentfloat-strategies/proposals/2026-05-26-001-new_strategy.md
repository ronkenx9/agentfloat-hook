# Introduce Mean Reversion

## Reasoning
The Mock Yield Strategy has shown high volatility, with a score range of 262,437 μbps. Introducing a mean reversion strategy could help stabilize returns and provide a more consistent performance. The Idle USDC Strategy serves as a baseline, but its low returns suggest there's room for a more dynamic approach. By introducing a mean reversion strategy, we can potentially capture more consistent returns and reduce the reliance on a single high-volatility strategy.

## Proposed change
Implement a mean reversion strategy that buys assets when they are below their historical mean and sells when they are above. This strategy will be designed to work in tandem with the existing Mock Yield Strategy, providing a more balanced approach to yield generation.

## Expected outcome
The introduction of a mean reversion strategy is expected to provide more consistent returns, reducing the overall volatility of the system. This should lead to a more stable and predictable performance, making it easier to manage risk and optimize yields.

The high volatility of the Mock Yield Strategy, as observed in the score log, suggests that a more stable approach is needed. The Idle USDC Strategy provides a baseline, but its low returns indicate that there's room for improvement. The introduction of a mean reversion strategy is based on the idea that assets tend to revert to their historical means, providing a more consistent and stable source of returns.

## Risks
